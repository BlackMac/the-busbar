lead:
  tag: Firmware
  headline: "SolarAssistant ships an official Home Assistant integration and opens its API — less reverse-engineering, less scraping"
  tl_dr: "SolarAssistant, the $15 one-time-license Raspberry Pi monitoring layer that sits on top of Deye, Growatt, EG4, Sol-Ark, and Voltronic-clone inverters, released v0.2.0 of an official Home Assistant integration on 2 July alongside a beta firmware build adding REST, WebSocket, and Cloud APIs — replacing a lot of the MQTT workarounds and custom decoders DIY builders have been hand-rolling for years."
  body: |
    <p><strong>SolarAssistant</strong> is not a brand most readers of this newsletter will have heard of, but a lot of them are already running it: it's a small independent monitoring layer, sold as a one-time <strong>$15</strong> license (two years of updates included, renewable for $14–24/year after), that runs on a Raspberry Pi and translates the proprietary RS485/serial protocols used by budget hybrid inverters — Deye, Growatt, EG4, Sol-Ark, MPP Solar and the wider family of Voltronic-clone units, SRNE, and others — into something a normal dashboard or automation system can use.</p>
    <p>On <strong>2 July</strong>, the project shipped two related things at once. First, <strong>v0.2.0</strong> of its official Home Assistant integration (its GitHub repository's second tagged release), which exposes live PV power, battery state of charge, grid and load power, voltage, current, and temperature as native Home Assistant entities over a local or cloud WebSocket connection — no separate MQTT broker required — plus cumulative energy totals that plug directly into HA's built-in Energy Dashboard. Second, a same-day <strong>2026-07-02 Beta</strong> firmware release for the SolarAssistant unit itself, adding REST, WebSocket, and Cloud APIs, a command-line tool, email notification automations, and expanded settings support across Growatt, SRNE, Sumry, Luxpower, Voltronic, Solis, and GoodWe inverters. It's explicitly a beta, not the stable channel.</p>
    <p>The context that makes this worth a Lead rather than a footnote: this newsletter has covered a steady stream of DIY builders writing their own monitoring dashboards specifically because vendor apps and BMS/inverter/shunt ecosystems don't talk to each other — an r/diysolar builder unifying three vendor apps into one dashboard in May, a German electric-boat builder hand-decoding motor telemetry onto a Victron Cerbo GX in last week's edition. An official, documented API on a monitoring layer this widely deployed under budget inverters removes a chunk of that reverse-engineering incentive at a stroke.</p>

community_pulse:
  body: |
    <p>A new-retiree thread on <strong>DIYSolarForum</strong>, "<strong>Solar Rook</strong>" (started 2 July by first-time poster ODawg), is worth following if you like watching a system get sized from scratch: the poster wants to run a 12×24 workshop (table saw, miter saw, vacuums, drills, mortiser) off-grid, started with a basic Harbor Freight Predator generator, and is now weighing a portable power station plus solar against a proper built bank — the exact decision point a lot of new off-grid builders hit before they understand duty cycles and surge loads.</p>
    <p>A second thread, "<strong>EG4 12kPV / 18kPV using grid to 'fill in' loads rather than battery</strong>" (poldim, DIYSolarForum, 4 July), is a useful config gotcha: the poster capped their System Charge SOC Limit at 80% to preserve battery life, then found short, heavy loads (microwave, coffee maker) pulling from the grid instead of the battery even with solar and load in steady state. Forum consensus: a BMS needs a periodic true 100% charge to balance cells, and capping SOC below that to save cycles can quietly break the balancing the BMS depends on — a different angle on the same "don't guess at 100%" theme this newsletter has covered before, but a concrete case of the setting that causes it.</p>
